# Editorial & Copy Standard

## 1. Objective

Copy must sound like a competent professional describing real work. It must be clear enough for non-technical readers and precise enough for technical readers.

English and Indonesian are authored independently from the same facts.

## 2. Voice

### English
- concise,
- direct,
- evidence-led,
- natural professional language,
- limited jargon,
- no startup-marketing tone.

Preferred:
“I coordinate project delivery, clarify requirements, follow up issues, and stay close enough to the technical work to understand what is actually blocking progress.”

Avoid:
“I leverage cross-functional synergies to deliver innovative, scalable, future-ready digital solutions.”

### Indonesian
Use natural Indonesian business/corporate language. Technical English terms may remain when they are the normal working term.

Preferred:
“Saya menangani koordinasi proyek, perumusan kebutuhan, tindak lanjut isu, dan monitoring progres, sambil tetap cukup dekat dengan sisi teknis untuk memahami hambatan yang terjadi.”

Avoid literal translation:
“Saya memanfaatkan sinergi lintas fungsi untuk memberikan solusi digital inovatif, terukur, dan siap masa depan.”

## 3. Translation rule

Meaning parity is required. Sentence parity is not.

Do:
- rewrite for natural local usage,
- preserve facts and intent,
- preserve named methods and technical terms when appropriate.

Do not:
- mirror grammar,
- force identical sentence length,
- translate familiar technical terms into awkward Indonesian,
- add claims in one locale that do not exist in the other.

## 4. Claims

Every factual claim must be one of:
- directly verifiable from portfolio data,
- backed by evidence,
- clearly attributed,
- a qualitative statement without invented precision.

Forbidden:
- fabricated performance metrics,
- fake satisfaction percentages,
- arbitrary skill percentages,
- invented team counts,
- exaggerated outcome language.

If an outcome cannot be quantified, state what changed operationally or technically without inventing a number.

## 5. Section writing contracts

### Home hero
Answer:
- who is this person,
- what is the current professional focus,
- what differentiates the work,
- what should the visitor open next.

Avoid stacking role labels without context.

### Work listing
Intro should explain the range of work without self-praise.

### Work detail
Prioritize:
- context,
- role,
- challenge,
- actions/decisions,
- evidence,
- outcome,
- lessons where useful.

Use only sections that add information.

### About
Explain career through-line and working approach. Do not write an autobiography.

### Insights
Open with the actual problem, question, or observation. Avoid generic thought-leadership introductions.

### Contact
Short, practical, and specific about what kinds of conversation are welcome.

## 6. Terminology baseline

Public English:
- Work
- Insights
- About
- Contact
- Resume
- Case study when the content truly is a case study

Public Indonesian:
- Karya or Pekerjaan may be used as the display label depending on context; route remains /work.
- Insight is acceptable; “Catatan” may be used for a more editorial tone.
- Tentang
- Kontak
- Resume / CV

Admin English may remain English-first for product consistency unless bilingual admin UI becomes a product requirement.

## 7. CTA rules

CTA labels describe the action:
- View work
- Read case study
- Open live site
- View repository
- Read insight
- Download resume
- Contact me

Avoid:
- Learn more everywhere
- Explore now
- Discover more
- Get started

Do not append arrows solely for decoration.

## 8. Microcopy

Helper copy must explain:
- what the field affects,
- what happens after an action,
- risk or irreversible consequences,
- publication/visibility effects.

Example:
“Changing a published permalink keeps the previous URL active with a permanent redirect.”

Not:
“Enter your slug here.”

## 9. AI-writing quality gate

Reject copy if it has:
- inflated abstraction,
- excessive em dashes,
- triads used repeatedly for rhythm,
- buzzwords without evidence,
- generic self-congratulation,
- vague “impact” wording,
- claims without source,
- unnatural Indonesian caused by literal translation.

The final copy should be understandable when read aloud.
